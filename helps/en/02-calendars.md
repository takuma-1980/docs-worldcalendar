# Chapter 2 Using Calendar Systems from Around the World

WorldCalendar lets you switch the calendar system used to display dates. Calendar systems count days and organize months differently, so the same day has a different year, month, and day in each system.

<a id="calendar-types"></a>

## Available calendar systems

### Six calendar systems

- **Gregorian calendar**: The calendar commonly used internationally.
- **Japanese lunisolar calendar**: Combines the phases of the Moon with the solar year. It is known as a historical calendar of Japan and other parts of East Asia.
- **Julian calendar**: A solar calendar widely used before the Gregorian calendar and still used in some church calendars.
- **Hebrew calendar**: Combines lunar and solar cycles and is used for Jewish observances and other purposes.
- **Indian lunisolar calendar (Amanta; Hindu calendar)**: An Indian calendar system displayed using the Amanta convention. Day numbers count elapsed days from the start of the month and differ from the tithi used in religious calendars.
- **Ethiopian calendar**: A calendar with 13 months used in Ethiopia and elsewhere.

The Hindu and Japanese lunisolar calendar displays may take a little time to load.

<a id="calendar-limits"></a>

## Calendar dates and calculation methods

Dates are displayed using the app's calculation methods. Calendar practices vary by region, religious tradition, and time zone, so displayed dates may differ from official local dates or religious determinations. The Indian lunisolar calendar uses the Amanta convention. The Hindu calendar does not offer a choice covering every regional custom or astronomical calculation for months and dates. For important observances, also check official information from the relevant region or organization.

### When the date changes

The app uses the following boundaries for date display.

| Calendar system | Date display boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Gregorian and Julian | Midnight in the device's time zone |
| Japanese lunisolar | Midnight in Japan time |
| Hebrew | Midnight in Jerusalem time (not sunset) |
| Indian lunisolar (Amanta) | Midnight in India time (different from sunrise or the tithi used in religious calendars) |
| Ethiopian | 6:00 a.m. in Addis Ababa time |

For timed events in the Ethiopian calendar, entering 00:00 corresponds to 06:00 in the device's time zone. Date display and event time entry use different reference points, so check event dates and times, especially when using different device time zones or near a date boundary.

### Calculation range and further reading

The range available in the year/month picker does not guarantee agreement with every regional calendar or historical date. Dates vary by calendar method and the treatment of leap months, so do not compare calendar systems using only their numeric year, month, and day values.

For the basic principles of calendars, see the [U.S. Naval Observatory's introduction to calendars](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/calendars). For differences between the Gregorian and Julian calendars, see the [National Astronomical Observatory of Japan's explanation](https://eco.mtk.nao.ac.jp/koyomi/topics/html/topics1994.html) (in Japanese). These are general explanations of calendar systems and do not guarantee the accuracy of this app's calculations.

## Switch the displayed calendar system

### Switch from the main screen

Tap the current calendar system name and “▼” at the top left of the main screen, then choose the system you want to display. Only enabled calendar systems are available. Date details show the date in the selected calendar system.

### Enable or disable calendar systems

1. Tap <img class="button-icon" src="../../assets/icons/settings.svg" width="20" height="20" alt="Gear"> (Settings) at the bottom of the main screen.
2. Open calendar display settings.
3. Change each calendar system's switch.

At least one calendar system must remain enabled. You can make the same selection on the calendar selection screen during initial setup.

<a id="calendar-order-color"></a>

## Display order and colors

### Change the order of calendar systems

Under <img class="button-icon" src="../../assets/icons/settings.svg" width="20" height="20" alt="Gear"> (Settings) > calendar display settings, touch and hold “=” (the reorder handle) at the right end of a calendar system's row, then drag it up or down. This order also applies to the calendar system choices on the main screen. You can also reorder them on the display order screen during initial setup.

### Change a calendar system's color

Under <img class="button-icon" src="../../assets/icons/settings.svg" width="20" height="20" alt="Gear"> (Settings) > calendar display settings, tap “●” (the color circle) in the desired calendar system's row. Choose a color on the color selection screen to save it and return to the previous screen. Colors help distinguish calendar systems. You cannot change the color of a calendar system without a color selector, or one unavailable in this build.

<a id="rokuyo-settings"></a>

## Rokuyo

### Display Rokuyo

Open <img class="button-icon" src="../../assets/icons/settings.svg" width="20" height="20" alt="Gear"> (Settings) > event management, then tap “☑ / ☐” (the checkbox) near the bottom of the screen for displaying Rokuyo in the Gregorian and Japanese lunisolar calendars. Rokuyo is supported in these two calendar systems and is not displayed in the others.

Rokuyo consists of Sensho, Tomobiki, Senbu, Butsumetsu, Taian, and Shakko. It is provided as calendar reference information. Regional calculation practices and calendar annotations may differ, so the display may not match other sources.
