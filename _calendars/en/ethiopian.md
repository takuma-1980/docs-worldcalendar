## Overview

Also known as the Ge’ez calendar, the Ethiopian calendar is a solar calendar distinguished by its short thirteenth month and its year numbering.

## Where it is used

It is used in everyday and cultural life in Ethiopia. Gregorian dates are also used in international communication, so naming the calendar helps avoid ambiguity.

## Historical background

It is closely related to the Coptic calendar. Its year numbering reflects a different chronology of the annunciation and birth of Jesus; it is not simply the Gregorian calendar with different month names.

## Years, months and days

The first twelve months have 30 days each. Pagume, the thirteenth month, has five days in a common year and six in a leap year. The year therefore contains 365 or 366 days.

## Compared with the Gregorian calendar

Month boundaries, the new year and year numbering differ. The year number is seven or eight behind the Gregorian year depending on the time of year, rather than a single fixed subtraction.

## Date calculation

The app uses Julian day number calculations and treats an Ethiopian year whose remainder on division by four is three as a leap year. Calendar arithmetic and the app’s chosen day boundary should be considered separately.

## Using it in WorldCalendar

Select Ethiopian from the calendar selector. Date display changes at 6 a.m. in Addis Ababa time. The month picker includes the thirteenth month.

## Limitations and important notes

Timed event entry instead uses the device time zone with a six-hour offset: an entered 00:00 corresponds to 06:00 on the device. This differs from the display boundary, so check the resulting event date and time.
