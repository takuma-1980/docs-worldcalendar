## Overview

The Gregorian calendar is a solar calendar: its year follows the seasonal cycle rather than keeping each month aligned with the Moon. It is the calendar most often used for everyday civil dates.

## Where it is used

It is widely used for international and civil purposes. Many communities also maintain other calendars for cultural, religious or everyday traditions.

## Historical background

Introduced in 1582, it reformed the Julian calendar to reduce its accumulated drift relative to the seasons. Adoption happened at different times in different places, which matters when interpreting historical records.

## Years, months and days

A year has 12 months and either 365 or 366 days. A year divisible by four is a leap year, except a century year must also be divisible by 400.

## Compared with the Gregorian calendar

It provides a familiar reference for comparing calendar systems. The start of a lunisolar month or year is not fixed to the same Gregorian date each year.

## Date calculation

Its arithmetic rules are predictable, but historical adoption sometimes involved skipped dates. Extending the rules backward in time is different from reproducing the calendar actually used in a particular place.
