## Overview

The Hebrew calendar is lunisolar: lunar months are adjusted to remain connected to the seasonal year. The modern fixed calendar determines dates through calculation rules.

## Where it is used

It is used in Israel and by Jewish communities around the world for observances and commemorations, often alongside the Gregorian calendar.

## Historical background

Calendar practice developed from observational methods toward a fixed calculated calendar. Applying modern rules backward is not the same as reconstructing every period of historical practice.

## Years, months and days

A common year has twelve months. Seven years within a 19-year cycle have an additional month. Month lengths and the total length of a year depend on the calendar’s rules, including those governing the new year.

## Compared with the Gregorian calendar

Year numbering, month structure and the new year differ from the Gregorian calendar. A fixed Hebrew month and day moves within the Gregorian calendar from year to year.

## Date calculation

The app orders months from Tishrei, using the civil-year sequence and calculated leap-month and new-year rules. Religious days begin at sunset, but the app uses midnight in Jerusalem for date display.

## Limitations and important notes

The app does not switch dates at sunset. It should not be used to determine religious start or end times or local religious rulings. Check official guidance from the relevant community or organization.
