## Overview

The Julian calendar is a solar calendar and the predecessor of the Gregorian calendar. Its months are not kept in step with lunar phases.

## Where it is used

It appears in historical records and remains relevant to some Christian liturgical calendars. Practices vary between churches and communities, so an observance cannot be inferred from the calendar name alone.

## Historical background

Julius Caesar introduced the reform in 45 BCE. Over time, the difference between its average year and the seasonal year accumulated, helping motivate the later Gregorian reform.

## Years, months and days

A year contains 12 months and 365 days, or 366 in a leap year. A leap day is added every four years, without the Gregorian rule that excludes some century years.

## Compared with the Gregorian calendar

The main distinction is the leap-year rule. The offset between matching Julian and Gregorian month-and-day labels changes over the centuries; it is not permanently fixed.

## Date calculation

The app converts dates using Julian day numbers. An arithmetic extension of the calendar should be distinguished from its actual historical implementation, particularly in its earliest years.
